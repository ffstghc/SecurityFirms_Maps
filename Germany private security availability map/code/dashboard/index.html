How to read this

Supply

People working in private guarding and security firms (WZ 2008 class 80.1, "Private Wach- und Sicherheitsdienste"), counted at their place of work: employees subject to social insurance plus marginal (minijob) employees, from the Federal Employment Agency, reference date 31 March 2026. It is total headcount, not event-security staff only: most of it is object protection, aviation security (strong in Hessen because of Frankfurt airport) and public-sector contracts (strong in Berlin), so a high value does not mean spare event capacity.

Demand

Annual visits to large, recurring crowd events with published figures, assigned to the state where they take place: professional football (1st–3rd league, season 2025/26), the 30 biggest Volksfeste listed by the German showmen's association (DSB, 2023), city festivals reported above one million visits, the largest trade and consumer fairs of 2025 and the ten biggest music festivals. Christmas markets, arena concerts, carnival parades, demonstrations and most smaller events are not included because no consistent per-state figures exist; states whose demand sits mostly in such events (for example Berlin, Saxony, Thuringia) are understated.

Metrics

  • Workers per 100k event visits: security workers in the state ÷ counted event visits × 100,000. Low = thin supply relative to event demand.
  • Index: the state's value relative to Germany (100). Below 100 = less supply per visit than average.
  • Workers per 100k residents: a demand-free check of how dense the security labour pool is.
  • Priority segments: "Thin on both" = below average per event visit and per resident — the clearest gap; "Thin per event visit" = many events but an average-or-better labour pool; "Thin per resident" = small labour pool but few counted events.

Workers cross state lines for big events and firms deploy staff nationally, so read the map as a relative indicator of where local supply is stretched, not as a staffing count.

How to read the Bavaria view

Supply

At district level the Federal Employment Agency does not publish industry class 80.1, so this page counts people working in security occupations (KldB 2010 main group 53 "Schutz-, Sicherheits-, Überwachungsberufe") at their place of work, insured employees plus minijobs, 31 March 2026. That is broader than the Germany page: it also includes in-house security, plant fire brigades and airport screeners, which is why Freising and Erding (Munich airport) stand out. Police officers are civil servants and are not included.

Demand

Bavarian events with published visitor figures: football leagues 1–3 and ice-hockey DEL (season 2025/26, home attendance), the Bavarian Volksfeste from the DSB list plus further large Volksfeste reported by BAYERN TOURISMUS and press, the Christmas markets in Munich and Nuremberg, the largest fairs of 2025 and the biggest music festivals. Basketball, arena concerts and smaller local fairs are not included. Most districts host none of these events, so their per-visit value is empty; switch to the 7 Regierungsbezirke to compare regions where staff can be drawn from neighbouring districts.

Reading it

Index 100 = Bavarian average for the selected event types. Low values mean few security workers in the area relative to the event visits it hosts. Big event cities draw staff from the surrounding districts, so pair the district view with the regional view.
